# Korean Food Glossary

## Core seasonings

**Gochujang (고추장)** — a thick, sweet-savoury fermented chilli paste made from
chilli powder, glutinous rice, fermented soybeans and salt. It is the backbone of
most Korean spicy dishes. Moderately hot rather than fiery, with a deep sweetness.
Contains soybeans and usually wheat.

**Gochugaru (고춧가루)** — coarse Korean chilli flakes, fruity and mild compared with
cayenne. Used in kimchi and stews.

**Doenjang (된장)** — fermented soybean paste, earthier and saltier than Japanese
miso. Used in stews and dipping sauces.

**Ganjang (간장)** — Korean soy sauce. Contains soybeans and wheat.

**Sesame oil (참기름)** — toasted, aromatic, added at the end of cooking rather than
used for frying.

## Dish types

**Banchan (반찬)** — the small shared side dishes served alongside a Korean meal.
Our name comes from these. A meal set typically includes two banchan.

**Bibimbap (비빔밥)** — literally "mixed rice". A bowl of rice topped with seasoned
vegetables, usually beef, a fried egg and gochujang, stirred together before eating.
*Dolsot* bibimbap is served in a hot stone bowl that crisps the rice at the base.

**Bulgogi (불고기)** — "fire meat". Thin slices of beef marinated in soy sauce, pear,
garlic and sesame, then grilled. Sweet and savoury rather than spicy.

**Jjigae (찌개)** — a thick, bubbling stew served communally. *Kimchi jjigae* is made
with aged, sour kimchi, tofu and pork. *Sundubu jjigae* uses uncurdled soft tofu and
is silkier and usually spicier.

**Tteok (떡)** — chewy rice cakes. *Tteokbokki* is cylindrical tteok simmered in a
sweet-spicy gochujang sauce; it is the definitive Korean street food.

**Japchae (잡채)** — stir-fried sweet potato glass noodles with vegetables, seasoned
with soy and sesame. Springy and slightly sweet. Naturally wheat-free, though the
soy seasoning contains gluten.

**Gimbap (김밥)** — rice and fillings rolled in seaweed and sliced. Often compared to
sushi, but the rice is seasoned with sesame oil rather than vinegar, and the
fillings are cooked rather than raw.

**Mandu (만두)** — Korean dumplings, pan-fried or steamed, usually filled with pork
and vegetables.

**Pajeon (파전)** — a savoury pancake dominated by spring onion. *Haemul pajeon* adds
seafood. Crisp at the edges, soft in the centre.

**Samgyeopsal (삼겹살)** — thick-cut pork belly, grilled and wrapped in lettuce with
garlic and ssamjang.

**Kimchi (김치)** — vegetables, most often napa cabbage, salted and fermented with
gochugaru, garlic and ginger. Sour, spicy and funky; it grows more sour with age,
and older kimchi is preferred for cooking.

## Sweets and drinks

**Sikhye (식혜)** — a sweet, cloudy rice punch served cold, traditionally after a
meal. Lightly sweet with whole grains of rice floating in it.

**Patbingsu (팥빙수)** — shaved ice topped with sweetened red bean, condensed milk
and often rice cake. Shared between two people.

**Hotteok (호떡)** — a griddled pancake filled with molten brown sugar, cinnamon and
crushed nuts. A winter street food, eaten hot.

## Spice levels

Korean heat is generally moderate and layered rather than sharp. Our spiciest
dishes are tteokbokki, sundubu stew and the sweet and spicy fried chicken. Bulgogi,
japchae, gimbap and the soy-garlic chicken are not spicy at all.
